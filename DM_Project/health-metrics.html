Mathematics Behind Health Metrics

Multivariable linear equations, scalar activity scaling, and numerical interval logic.

Discrete Math Model
1. Mifflin-St Jeor Transform
\( f(W, H, A, s) = 10W + 6.25H - 5A + s \)

BMR is formulated as a linear scalar function over the domain of physical parameters \( (W, H, A, s) \in \mathbb{R}^3 \times \{+5, -161\} \).

2. Activity Multiplier Mapping
\( \text{TDEE} = k \cdot \text{BMR}, \quad k \in \{1.2, 1.375, 1.55, 1.725, 1.9\} \)

Maps discrete activity level enums to scalar multipliers in finite set \( K \).

3. Heart Rate Interval Logic
\( \text{HR}_{\max} = 220 - A \implies Z_1 = [0.5 \cdot \text{HR}_{\max}, 0.7 \cdot \text{HR}_{\max}] \)

Partitions continuous pulse spectrum into discrete target training zones.
