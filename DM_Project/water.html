Mathematics Used

Discrete summations, bounded ratio scaling, set partitions, and descriptive statistics.

Discrete Math Model
1. Intake Summation
\( I_{\text{today}} = \sum_{j=1}^{m} w_j \)

Today's total volume \( I \) is the finite summation of all water entry amounts logged on the current local date.

2. Bounded Progress Function
\( P(I, T) = \min\left(100, \left\lfloor\frac{I}{T} \times 100\right\rceil\right) \)

Maps actual intake \( I \ge 0 \) relative to target \( T > 0 \) into a clamped progress percentage in \( [0, 100] \).

3. Non-negative Difference
\( R(I, T) = \max(0, T - I) \)

Remaining volume \( R \) is constrained to non-negative real numbers to prevent negative deficit values.

4. Set Theory Model
\( W_{\text{today}} = \{ w \in W_{\text{history}} \mid \text{date}(w) = \text{localKey}() \} \subset W_{\text{history}} \)

Today's entries form a discrete subset \( W_{\text{today}} \) partitioned from the universal set of all recorded entries \( W_{\text{history}} \).

5. Statistical Indicators
\( \mu = \frac{\sum_{k=1}^N D_k}{N}, \quad \text{Max} = \max(D), \quad \text{Min} = \min(D) \)

Computes the arithmetic mean, maximum, minimum, and total cardinality \( N \) across all unique daily intake totals.
